<StorySection number="02" title="Three ways in" id="structure">
  <p slot="problem">Merchants come to StartHub with different intents: the most common scenario is to search for a product they heard about, but some are also open to be inspired and discover new proucts. Offering a direct channel to vote on what their region offers is another new entrypoint.</p>
  <p slot="decision">We structured the shop around these three intents: a prominent product search across the whole product range, a start page and categories for discovery, and a separate area for voting.</p>

<StorySection number="03" title="Decision data first" id="decision-data">
  <p slot="problem">A merchant decides with numbers: purchase price, recommended retail price, margin, order unit, and how a product sells in other stores. A beautiful product page alone doesn't help them decide.</p>
  <p slot="decision">Hovering a product tile shows price, margin and average sales; the product page leads with them, followed by sell-out data per region and the full master data, with article numbers ready to copy.</p>
  <p slot="outcome">Merchants can judge a product before the first click, and the story of the brand and its team supports the numbers instead of replacing them.</p>

<StorySection number="04" title="Orders per store" id="stores">
  <p slot="problem">Merchants often order for several stores, and some users work for more than one. A sample or a vote that lands at the wrong store is useless.</p>
  <p slot="decision">Every action that has an effect in a store asks for the store, but only when there is a choice. Sample orders go to a selected store address, and voting asks which stores the vote is for only when the user works for several.</p>
  <p slot="outcome">Merchants with one store never see the extra step, and those with several stay in control.</p>

<StorySection number="05" title="Giving merchants a say" id="voting">
  <p slot="problem">Whether a trend product is offered in a region is decided by a regional buying committee. Merchants had no direct way to influence this, and the StartHub team had little insight into what merchants actually wanted.</p>
  <p slot="decision">I designed voting as a second area next to the shop. The Trend Team presents products per region, merchants vote with "I would order this", comment, and suggest products of their own. Each product shows its status: newly presented, in voting, accepted or not accepted.</p>
  <p slot="outcome">Merchant votes inform the committee's decision, and accepted products become orderable in the shop. The StartHub team gets a direct view of merchants' wishes per region.</p>

<StorySection number="06" title="Bringing ideas inside" id="start-page">
  <p slot="problem">The impulses that make merchants look for a product happen outside the portal, so StartHub was rarely where an idea started.</p>
  <p slot="decision">I designed a start page that collects these impulses in one place: what's new, picks from the shop, current votes and regional products. I explored whether news should reuse the shop tiles or get a lighter variant of its own.</p>
  <p slot="outcome">The start page shipped as the new entry point. Curated editorial content was outlined as the next step, beyond what the team could resource at the time.</p>

    <Figure gallery src={screen} alt="Start page with an introduction, news, picks from the shop, current votes and regional products" caption="The start page" />

<StorySection number="07" title="Shipping step by step" id="shipping">
  <p slot="problem">The old portal was in daily use, and the team had two developers. A big-bang relaunch was not an option.</p>
  <p slot="decision">We moved the whole portal into the new design and structure step by step. I kept the order process close to the existing one, with quality-of-life improvements, and moved a restructured, cart-like ordering experience to the roadmap: valuable, but too expensive for the team at the time and not our main goal.</p>
  <p slot="outcome">Merchants could keep ordering throughout, while discovery and voting got the team's attention first.</p>
