<Chapter title="From calls to meetings" id="framing">
  Calls already exist in Wire, but always instantly and within the context of an existing group. I searched for better terminology to explain the planned changes. By framing the feature as Meetings, we made clear that a call is just the medium, while a meeting reflects the user's actual goal: aligning with people, whether instantly or scheduled, within a group or without one. Getting this terminology right from the start helped set expectations with both users and stakeholders, and helped communicate the added user value.

<Chapter title="Design solution" id="solution">
  <Fragment slot="media">
    <SubChapter number={1} title="Instant meetings" id="instant">
      Users want to start a call without creating a channel or group first, an experience that is established by the main competitors (Zoom, Meet and Slack). As people can also join a meeting without a Wire account at all (if the host allows it), instant meetings are an important growth driver.

      <ChallengeSolution>
        <p slot="challenge">Wire's existing "invite guests" flow had already shown critical UX issues in previous user testing. For example, a new link must be generated whenever the password changes, and the password can't be viewed again once set.</p>
        <p slot="solution">I wasn't able to get buy-in to rework the invitation flow within the timeline of this project, so I had to design around the existing constraints to not block the team. However, I moved the invitation-link improvements to next quarter's roadmap.</p>
      </ChallengeSolution>

    <SubChapter number={2} title="Scheduled meetings" id="scheduled">
      Scheduled meetings typically happen within existing groups, as part of ongoing team collaboration, which is why scheduling ahead of time is essential. Users need to stay informed about upcoming meetings without adding to information overload, and be able to manage them as effortlessly as possible.

      <ChallengeSolution>
        <p slot="challenge">Finding the best timeslot for each participant without excessive back-and-forth is critical for scheduling a meeting, and synced calendars are key to finding that slot. However, building a full calendar replacement was considered too costly, especially as users' willingness to switch to yet another calendar was uncertain.</p>
        <p slot="solution">Our approach for the first implementation was to focus on a basic list view of upcoming meetings. This lets users handle the core functionality (scheduling, joining, accepting or declining, and getting an overview) directly within the app. A Wire meeting plugin for Outlook and Gmail is planned as the next milestone.</p>
      </ChallengeSolution>

    <SubChapter number={3} title="Calling experience" id="calling">
      Once a meeting has started, the calling experience defines how users interact with each other. Since this is the most prominent and visible part of the feature, I made sure to include additional improvements here as well.

      <ChallengeSolution>
        <p slot="challenge">Unlike the main competitors, Wire encrypts every video stream end-to-end. This means that each device must decrypt the incoming streams itself, limiting how many videos can be shown at once (max. 9 tiles). The current implementation adds pagination for calls with more than 9 participants, but this makes big calls confusing as it can hide active speakers on another page.</p>
        <p slot="solution">In a workshop with engineers, we developed a new approach: participants are ordered by speaking activity. Active speakers fill the nine visible tiles, while passive listeners move into an overflow slot and reappear as soon as they speak again. This removes pagination entirely and keeps every call on a single, clean screen. To demonstrate how it works, I vibe-coded the prototype below.</p>
      </ChallengeSolution>
