<StorySection number="01" title="Key user insights" id="insights">
  I extracted insights from stakeholder and peer interviews (Sales, Customer Success, Product and others), segment research and qualitative user interviews.

  <Fragment slot="media">
    <ImageText image={research1} alt="Illustration of familiar meeting patterns" title="Familiar patterns">

    </ImageText>
    <ImageText image={research2} alt="Illustration of temporary and persistent groups" title="Temporary vs. persistent">

<StorySection number="02" title="From calls to meetings" id="framing">
  <p slot="problem">Calls already exist in Wire, but always instantly and within the context of an existing group. The terminology didn't explain the planned changes.</p>
  <p slot="decision">I framed the feature as Meetings. A call is just the medium, while a meeting reflects the user's actual goal: aligning with people, whether instantly or scheduled, within a group or without one.</p>
  <p slot="outcome">Getting the terminology right from the start helped set expectations with both users and stakeholders, and helped communicate the added user value.</p>

  <Fragment slot="media">
    <Figure src={framing} alt="Overview of how calls become meetings: instant or scheduled, with or without a group" />
  </Fragment>
</StorySection>

<StorySection number="03" title="Instant meetings" id="instant">
  <p slot="problem">Users want to start a call without creating a channel or group first. Wire's existing "invite guests" flow had already shown critical UX issues in user testing: a new link must be generated whenever the password changes, and the password can't be viewed again once set.</p>
  <p slot="decision">I wasn't able to get buy-in to rework the invitation flow within the timeline, so I designed around the existing constraints to not block the team, and moved the invitation-link improvements to next quarter's roadmap.</p>
  <p slot="outcome">Meetings can be started instantly, as established by Zoom, Meet and Slack. People can join without a Wire account if the host allows it, which makes instant meetings an important growth driver.</p>

<StorySection number="04" title="Scheduled meetings" id="scheduled">
  <p slot="problem">Scheduled meetings happen within existing groups. Finding the best timeslot without excessive back-and-forth is critical, and synced calendars are key to that, but a full calendar replacement was too costly and users' willingness to switch to yet another calendar was uncertain.</p>
  <p slot="decision">I focused the first implementation on a basic list view of upcoming meetings, so users stay informed without adding to information overload.</p>
  <p slot="outcome">Users handle the core functionality (scheduling, joining, accepting or declining, and getting an overview) directly within the app. A Wire meeting plugin for Outlook and Gmail is planned as the next milestone.</p>

<StorySection number="05" title="Calling experience" id="calling">
  <p slot="problem">Wire encrypts every video stream end-to-end, so each device must decrypt incoming streams itself, limiting the call to 9 tiles. The existing pagination for bigger calls can hide active speakers on another page.</p>
  <p slot="decision">In a workshop with engineers, we developed a new approach: participants are ordered by speaking activity. Active speakers fill the nine visible tiles, while passive listeners move into an overflow slot and reappear as soon as they speak again. I vibe-coded the prototype below to demonstrate it.</p>
  <p slot="outcome">Pagination is gone and every call stays on a single, clean screen.</p>
