<Chapter title="Starting situation" id="situation">
  Based on an extensive research phase among our users, we identified three pains and gains.

  <Fragment slot="media">
    <ImageText image={survey1} alt="Survey result: 78.5 % of 219 respondents have compared the energy use of two different days" title="Compare history">
      Users want to compare their historical data to identify behavioural patterns, so that they can save energy and money.
    </ImageText>
    <ImageText image={survey2} alt="Survey result about comparing oneself to others" title="Compare to others">
      Users want to compare themselves to meaningful others, so that they better understand the normal and how they perform.
    </ImageText>
    <ImageText image={survey3} alt="Survey result about trust in the data provided" title="Transparency">
      Transparency and traceability of the data provided are very important to users.
    </ImageText>
  </Fragment>
</Chapter>

<Chapter title="The task" id="task">
  My task was to build an interface that lets users understand large amounts of consumption data, gain insights from it and enjoy a frictionless experience.

  To get there, I gathered the ideas and insights circulating in the team, helped the team sync and ideate on them, and came up with a prototype to test our assumptions.
</Chapter>

<Chapter title="Design sprint" id="sprint">
  The design team, a freelance designer and I, facilitated a remote design sprint over two days. At the end, we voted for our favourite idea, "My Energy Space in the World".

  Due to time constraints, the prototype and the validation phase were separate from the sprint.

<Chapter title="Designing the prototype" id="prototype">
  After writing down a basic user flow and laying out the information structure, I translated the favourite idea into a conceptual mock. From there, I refined it screen by screen until the prototype was ready for testing.

  A key aspect was to show a user's consumption in relation to their group of meaningful others, and the distribution of consumption within that group. It was a very data-driven concept.

<Chapter title="Visualising the data" id="charts">
  I iterated on different chart types: histograms for the distribution, line and bucket charts for monthly consumption, and slider charts for the relation of a user to their group. I used real data from the data analysis team to stay as close as possible to our users' real-life scenarios.

<Chapter title="Validation" id="validation">
  One week after the sprint, we conducted qualitative interviews with five users and documented the findings as the base for the final iteration.

  The most interesting finding: four of five users were not interested in the distribution of consumption in their group. They liked comparing themselves with others as a starting point, but their main interest was comparing their own historical data to save energy, which they would do daily or several times a week.
</Chapter>

<Chapter title="Implementation" id="implementation">
  The implementation was put on hold, as the team needed to focus on other functionality first. I created the final design based on our findings, so we can pick it up once the roadmap allows.
