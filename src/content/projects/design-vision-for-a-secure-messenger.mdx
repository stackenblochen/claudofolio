<StorySection number="03" title="Vision and principles" id="principles">
  I wrote a vision statement and tied it to the new product vision, "highest security for people who depend on it". Features like a global search, a new navigation and the most critical flows became part of it, but a vision needs balance. Look and feel and onboarding shape a product's personality more directly than features do.

<StorySection number="04" title="A personality layer" id="personality">
  The central idea is a minimal frame around the app, a layer the UI sits on. It carries the personality and can project any tonality, such as Wire branded, calm or playful, and fits the accent color theming we already had. The UI itself stays minimal and focused, so the personality never gets in the way of productivity. Users can customize it to make the app their own.

  I built a web prototype with AI support, based on shadcn, and then applied the idea to iOS and Android, where it worked just as well. The vision builds on a unified multiplatform design system that all three platforms share, which I cover in <a href={siteUrl('/projects/managing-a-multiplatform-design-system/')}>a separate case study</a>.

<StorySection number="05" title="Getting it built" id="implementation">
  I laid out several ways to implement the vision, from a full rewrite to a step-by-step approach alongside feature work. A deep dive with engineers settled the picture: iOS already had a rewrite planned, Android could adopt the new design system easily, and the legacy-ridden web app would need a full rewrite. That decision was still open when I left.
