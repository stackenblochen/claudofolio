<StorySection number="01" title="Key insights" id="insights">
  These insights shaped the starting point of the vision. I gathered them from internal and external interviews, sector research, competitor analysis and a survey among our users.

<StorySection number="04" title="Design solution" id="design-solution">
  The core idea is a more consistent UI built on a unified design system, the foundation of which already existed (see <a href={siteUrl('/projects/managing-a-multiplatform-design-system/')}>this</a> case study). Native iOS and Material patterns for core flows and shadcn as a base for desktop give the visual layer a clear lift, and the existing color tokens make this an easy first step. On top of that, a background layer lets people personalize the app, with the layout adapted accordingly, especially on desktop. Feature-wise, the most requested improvements were global search and better ways to organize and filter conversations.

<StorySection number="05" title="Getting it built" id="implementation">
  I laid out several ways to implement the vision, from a full rewrite to a step-by-step approach alongside feature work. A deep dive with engineers settled the picture: iOS already had a rewrite planned, Android could adopt the new design system easily, and the legacy desktop app would need a full rewrite.

  I presented the vision during an all-hands meeting to the company, which kicked off an internal testing and feedback phase, with feedback from selected customers planned next.

<StorySection number="06" title="Outcome" id="outcome" closing>
  1. Design, product and leadership agreed on what 'modern' means for Wire, which gave the vision a shared direction.
  2. The personality layer showed how Wire can stay calm and focused and still carry a brand, on all three platforms. Early tests inside the company were positive.
  3. The vision made the case for a desktop rewrite concrete. With AI-assisted development, that rewrite now looks achievable within the team's capacity.
</StorySection>
